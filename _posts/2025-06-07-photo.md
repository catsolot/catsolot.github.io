<p style="text-align: center">
This blog post will introduce an approach to photowalking I've been experimenting with recently.
I have always liked taking long walks.
Being on foot immerses you in space and allows you freedom to explore details in a unique way.
As David Lynch notes in <i>The Art Life</i>: "Huge worlds are in those two blocks," pointing to the fractal
    complexity of just a small amount of space.
</p>

<p style="text-align: center">
In addition to walks, I also am fascinated with the concept of disposable cameras.
We have just a few shots to take before the camera is used up.
What do we capture?
Instant cameras have a similar allure, but with the benefit of instant feedback.
</p>

<div style="width: 50%; margin: auto; font-size: 1rem">
<img src="../../../img/photowalk/3.webp">
</div>
<br>

<p style="text-align: center">
In an effort to capture the feeling of working with limited film cameras and to bring out the complexities of the world
    only seen on foot, I have been experimenting with an approach to photowalking.
There are only two rules: everything must be taken in one shot, and shots should be chronological.
Of course rules are meant to be broken, but this gives us a framework fall back on.
</p>

<p style="text-align: center">
A walk is a series of moments connected together by our footsteps.
Single shots force us to be in the moment.
Instead of overthinking, we are called to action!
This is a perfectly imperfect medium.
</p>

<div style="width: 50%; margin: auto; font-size: 1rem">
<img src="../../../img/photowalk/6.webp">
</div>
<br>

<p style="text-align: center">
As a walk evolves, we too evolve.
The things we think about and notice change continuously.
The chronological ordering of shots allows our evolution to be captured.
</p>

<p style="text-align: center">
The end result is a single representation of what it was like to take a walk in a certain place, at a certain time.
We are forced to inhabit our past self when stepping through photos.
Each photo may be captioned with a description or other caption, but this is not a strict requirement.
</p>

<p style="text-align: center">
Through these photowalks we use our camera and our feet to dig, as Seamus Heaney uses his pen.
What will we find?
</p>
